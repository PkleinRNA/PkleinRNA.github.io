**Title**:  
Reaction-induced selection of phase-separated RNA protocells 

**Abstract**:  
The earliest Darwinian entities likely coupled self-reproducing molecules to protocell survival. However, such coupling has not been demonstrated for RNA-based protocells. Here, we show that ribozyme-catalyzed RNA reactions drive the differential survival of coacervate droplets. Sequence-dependent reactions generate distinct RNA length distributions, which shift coacervate phase boundaries. Consequently, a salinity increase induced during partial drying selectively dissolves less stable droplets, whereas droplets enriched in longer RNA products persist. Upon rehydration, surviving droplets regrow while retaining part of their previous RNA composition, alongside the formation of new droplets. These results establish a direct link between RNA sequence, catalytic activity, and compartment survival, and show that selection can be combined with compositional inheritance in RNA-based coacervates. They provide a minimal physicochemical route by which RNA reactions could generate protocell-level Darwinian properties.
 


<span style="font-size: 1.5em;"><strong>Arnaud Liehrmann </strong></span><br>
Postdoctoral Researcher in Élodie Laine's team at CQSB (Sorbonne Université)

**Title**:
phyloDS: phylogeny-aware differential splicing across species, beyond reference annotations

**Abstract**:  
Alternative splicing diverges rapidly between species, yet its comparative analysis lags far behind that of gene expression: junction orthology is defined on a single reference genome, species are treated as independent samples, and detected shifts are rarely grounded in sequence-level evidence. We present phyloDS, an RNA-seq–driven framework that addresses all three limitations. phyloDS defines orthologous splice junctions on multiple sequence alignments of one-to-one orthologous genes, tests their differential usage with a regularized Ornstein–Uhlenbeck model, and cross-validates detected shifts against splice-site predictions made from genomic sequence alone. Applied to primate brain and rodent heart transcriptomes, phyloDS recovered up to 2.9-fold more shared orthologous splice junctions than a reference-based strategy, and kept type I error close to the nominal level, whereas competing methods inflated it more than two-fold. It identified 234 and 502 differentially used orthologous splice junctions in hominoids and mole-rats, respectively (86 and 141 of them unannotated), revealing a mitochondrial and contractile splicing signature in mole-rats.
